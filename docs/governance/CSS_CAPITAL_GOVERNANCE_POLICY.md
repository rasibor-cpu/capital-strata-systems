CSS Capital Governance Policy

Project: Capital Strata Systems (CSS)
Branch: phase71-church-governance-pack
Version: 1.0
Status: Draft for Governance Approval

---

1. Purpose

This document establishes the Capital Governance Policy for Capital Strata Systems (CSS).

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that capital preservation remains the highest operational priority and that all deployment, allocation, and utilization of capital occurs within approved governance controls.

The primary objective of CSS is not maximum profit.

The primary objective of CSS is sustainable capital growth through controlled risk governance and controlled compounding.

---

2. Core Capital Principles

All capital decisions shall be governed by the following principles:

1. Capital Preservation First
2. Controlled Risk Governance
3. Controlled Compounding
4. Governance Before Performance
5. Sustainability Before Aggression
6. Survivability Before Optimization

No trading opportunity shall override capital governance requirements.

---

3. Capital Authority Hierarchy

Capital authority shall be exercised in the following order:

1. Governance Policy
2. Capital Governor
3. Risk Governor
4. Orchestrator
5. Strategy Layer
6. Execution Layer

If a conflict exists, the higher authority prevails.

---

4. Capital Categories

CSS shall maintain separate governance treatment for:

Paper Capital

Purpose:

- Development
- Testing
- Certification
- Validation

Paper capital shall never be treated as authoritative capital.

---

Live Capital

Purpose:

- Real-world deployment
- Controlled capital growth

Live capital shall be sourced from verified broker balances.

Synthetic balances shall not be used.

---

5. Capital Source of Truth

The authoritative source of capital shall be:

- Verified broker balances
- Approved accounting records

The following shall not be treated as authoritative:

- Static balances
- Hardcoded balances
- Placeholder balances
- Simulated balances during live operation

---

6. Capital Allocation Policy

Capital allocation shall consider:

- Available capital
- Risk budget
- Existing exposure
- Asset-class allocation
- Broker allocation
- Portfolio concentration

No allocation decision shall bypass governance controls.

---

7. Portfolio Allocation Limits

Capital shall be diversified across approved asset classes.

Target allocation limits shall be configurable through governance policy.

Illustrative limits:

- FX
- Crypto
- Futures
- Options
- Equities

No single asset class shall dominate portfolio exposure without explicit governance approval.

---

8. Position Sizing Governance

Position sizing shall consider:

- Available capital
- Portfolio exposure
- Asset-class risk
- Volatility
- Existing commitments

Position sizing shall remain subject to Capital Governor approval.

---

9. Risk Budget Governance

Every trade shall consume part of a defined risk budget.

Risk budget consumption shall be tracked and monitored.

When risk budgets are exhausted:

- New positions shall be restricted
- Additional allocation shall be prohibited

until governance conditions permit expansion.

---

10. Drawdown Protection

Capital protection controls shall include:

- Daily drawdown limits
- Weekly drawdown limits
- Monthly drawdown limits
- Portfolio drawdown limits

Exceeding approved thresholds may trigger:

- Position restrictions
- Defensive mode
- Kill-switch activation

---

11. Capital Governor Responsibilities

The Capital Governor shall:

- Validate available capital
- Approve allocation requests
- Enforce risk budgets
- Enforce exposure limits
- Prevent unauthorized deployment
- Protect portfolio survivability

The Capital Governor shall possess veto authority over trade deployment.

---

12. Live Deployment Requirements

Live capital deployment requires:

- Governance approval
- Security approval
- Certification completion
- Verified broker balances
- Deployment readiness approval

Live deployment shall not occur solely because signals exist.

---

13. Capital Protection Events

The following events shall trigger immediate governance review:

- Unexpected drawdowns
- Broker balance anomalies
- Capital mismatches
- Allocation failures
- Exposure breaches
- Kill-switch activation

---

14. Audit Requirements

All capital decisions shall be auditable.

Required records include:

- Allocation decisions
- Position sizing decisions
- Risk budget consumption
- Capital governor actions
- Capital restriction events

---

15. Success Definition

Capital governance is considered successful when:

- Capital is preserved during adverse conditions
- Portfolio survivability is maintained
- Risk remains controlled
- Governance controls remain effective
- Long-term compounding is achieved without compromising capital integrity

The success of CSS shall be measured not only by returns but by the preservation and disciplined growth of capital.
