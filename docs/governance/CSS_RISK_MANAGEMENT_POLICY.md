CSS Risk Management Policy

Project: Capital Strata Systems (CSS)
Branch: phase71-church-governance-pack
Version: 1.0
Status: Draft for Governance Approval

---

1. Purpose

This document establishes the Risk Management Policy for Capital Strata Systems (CSS).

The purpose of this policy is to ensure that risk remains measurable, controlled, monitored, and governed at all times.

The objective of CSS is not merely to generate returns.

The objective of CSS is to generate sustainable returns while preserving capital and maintaining portfolio survivability.

---

2. Risk Governance Principles

All risk decisions shall be governed by the following principles:

1. Capital Preservation First
2. Survivability Before Profitability
3. Governance Before Optimization
4. Diversification Before Concentration
5. Controlled Risk Taking
6. Measured Expansion
7. Continuous Monitoring

No trading opportunity shall override approved risk controls.

---

3. Risk Authority Hierarchy

Risk authority shall operate in the following order:

1. Governance Policy
2. Risk Governor
3. Capital Governor
4. Orchestrator
5. Strategy Layer
6. Execution Layer

Higher authority always overrides lower authority.

---

4. Risk Categories

Market Risk

Exposure arising from adverse market movement.

Liquidity Risk

Exposure arising from inability to enter or exit positions efficiently.

Execution Risk

Exposure arising from failed or incomplete order execution.

Broker Risk

Exposure arising from broker failure, outage, restrictions, or insolvency.

Technology Risk

Exposure arising from software, infrastructure, runtime, or connectivity failures.

Concentration Risk

Exposure arising from excessive allocation to:

- One asset
- One strategy
- One broker
- One asset class

---

5. Risk Budget Framework

Every position shall consume part of a defined risk budget.

Risk budgets shall be tracked at:

- Position Level
- Asset Level
- Asset Class Level
- Portfolio Level

When risk budgets are exhausted:

- New entries may be restricted
- Position expansion may be restricted
- Defensive mode may activate

---

6. Position Risk Limits

Position sizing shall consider:

- Available capital
- Volatility
- Existing exposure
- Correlation
- Asset-class risk

No position shall exceed approved governance limits.

---

7. Portfolio Risk Limits

Portfolio controls shall include:

- Maximum portfolio exposure
- Maximum drawdown
- Maximum concentration
- Maximum leverage
- Maximum correlation exposure

These limits shall be configurable through governance policy.

---

8. Asset-Class Risk Controls

Risk shall be monitored separately for:

- FX
- Cryptocurrency
- Futures
- Options
- Equities
- ETFs

Each asset class may maintain independent limits and thresholds.

---

9. Drawdown Protection

The following shall be monitored continuously:

- Daily Drawdown
- Weekly Drawdown
- Monthly Drawdown
- Lifetime Drawdown

Threshold breaches may trigger:

- Trade restrictions
- Allocation reductions
- Defensive mode
- Kill-switch activation

---

10. Concentration Risk Controls

CSS shall monitor:

- Single Asset Exposure
- Single Broker Exposure
- Single Strategy Exposure
- Single Asset-Class Exposure

Diversification shall be encouraged wherever practical.

---

11. Correlation Risk Controls

CSS shall evaluate:

- Asset correlation
- Strategy correlation
- Portfolio correlation

Highly correlated exposures may be restricted.

---

12. Risk Governor Responsibilities

The Risk Governor shall:

- Monitor portfolio risk
- Monitor drawdowns
- Monitor concentration
- Monitor leverage
- Monitor exposure
- Restrict unauthorized risk expansion

The Risk Governor shall possess veto authority over trade deployment.

---

13. Escalation Levels

Green

Normal operation.

Yellow

Elevated risk detected.

Enhanced monitoring required.

Orange

Material risk detected.

Restrictions may apply.

Red

Critical risk detected.

Defensive actions and kill-switch activation may occur.

---

14. Audit Requirements

All risk decisions shall be auditable.

Required records include:

- Risk calculations
- Drawdown events
- Exposure events
- Restriction events
- Kill-switch activations
- Governor interventions

---

15. Success Definition

Risk management is considered successful when:

- Capital is preserved
- Drawdowns remain controlled
- Portfolio survivability is maintained
- Risk remains within approved limits
- Governance controls remain effective

The objective of CSS is not the elimination of risk.

The objective of CSS is the disciplined governance of risk.
